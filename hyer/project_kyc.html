Know Your Commodity.

A live commodity-intelligence platform for Indian agriculture: government mandi prices, market analysis, and a 14-day price predictor that is honest about its uncertainty.

01 Overview

A live agri-market platform, with Dhairya Pareek leading the technology.

Know Your Commodity is an agri-market intelligence platform for traders, agri-businesses and market operators. It combines independent coverage of crop markets, trade and policy with live government mandi prices and a premium price predictor.

Dhairya Pareek leads the technology: the price data pipeline, the forecasting service, the AI explanation layer and the product itself. The platform is live at kycagri.com.

02 Problem

Mandi prices are public but hard to use, and a confident wrong forecast does damage.

India publishes wholesale mandi prices through Agmarknet, but the raw data is hard to use: thousands of markets, inconsistent coverage, and gaps where markets do not report. A trader deciding whether to buy or hold wants a simple answer about direction over the next one to two weeks.

The risk runs the other way too. A price forecast that looks more certain than it is can do real damage, so the system had to be useful without overstating what it knows.

03 What was built

Ingestion, a separate forecasting service, AI explanations and the product.

01

Price ingestion

Scheduled pulls from the Agmarknet dataset via the data.gov.in API, stored as daily snapshots and queryable by commodity, state, district, market, variety and grade.

02

Forecasting service

A separate service keeps the heavy data work out of the web app. Each forecast fits adaptive Holt’s exponential smoothing, choosing between 20 smoothing settings by walk-forward error on the most recent week, then projects 14 days ahead with an uncertainty band that widens with the horizon.

Honest by design

A predictor that admits its limits.

Every forecast response states the model used, carries a disclaimer, and reports how much of its input was backfilled where real market records were sparse. The interface is not allowed to hide any of it. A predictor that admits its limits is more useful than one that pretends not to have any.

03

AI explanations

A language model turns each forecast into a plain-language read of the trend and what is driving it. It runs through Unwind AI on open-weight Llama models, behind server-side routes so no keys reach the browser.

04

Product and access

A Next.js app serves the editorial content and price data, with the predictor behind a premium plan. Sign-in uses httpOnly cookies, every write is validated, and sensitive endpoints are rate-limited.

06 What we’d do differently

Held-out accuracy, visible gaps, richer inputs, freshness monitoring.

Measure accuracy on held-out data

The error figure shown alongside each forecast is measured on the same data the model was fitted to, so it flatters the model. A proper backtest on held-out weeks, per commodity, would give buyers a number they can rely on.

Show gaps instead of filling them

Where markets stop reporting, the service backfills to keep the series continuous, and says so. Today we would sooner widen the band or pool nearby markets than generate data at all.

Give the model more to see

Trend smoothing only sees past prices. Arrival volumes, weather and policy changes move mandi prices too; the next step is a model that uses them, with the current method kept as the baseline it has to beat.

Treat data freshness as a feature

The most common production problem was not the model but stale or missing data upstream. Freshness monitoring and visible “last updated” status should have been there from day one.
