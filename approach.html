How we work

From a 30-minute call to a system in production.

How projects run, the ways to start, and answers to the questions clients usually ask before getting in touch.

Process

Four steps on every project.

The first two steps happen before any code is written.

01

Clarify the real problem

Identify constraints, stakeholders, risks and hidden assumptions before a line of code is written. Requirements that aren’t understood become bugs that are expensive to fix.

02

Design a lean MVP architecture

Define interfaces, data flows and AI boundaries before building. A well-designed architecture makes every subsequent decision faster and less risky.

03

Build for scale and observability

Structure systems for modularity, iteration and debuggability from day one. Systems that can’t be observed in production can’t be reliably improved.

04

Deliver, validate and refine

Continuous validation through Agile and requirements engineering. The best systems improve after deployment, so validation continues past launch.

Engagements

Three ways to start, smallest first.

Most projects begin with a scoped first step, so you see the thinking and the plan before committing to a full build. You work with the engineers who design and build the system, not an account manager.

Start with a 30-minute call
01, where most projects start

Scoping and architecture

We map the workflow, the systems it touches, the compliance boundaries and the risks, and hand over a written architecture and delivery plan. You can build it with us, with your own team, or decide it is not worth building.

02

Pilot, then production

A first version on one high-value workflow, measured against how it runs today. Once it proves itself, it is rolled out fully with CRM and tool integration, logging and monitoring.

03

Senior support for your team

Architecture reviews and hands-on engineering alongside your developers, for an AI system you already run or are partway through building.

Questions

Six questions clients ask before writing in.

Anything not covered here is worth a message.

Who will we actually work with?

Krish Shah and Dhairya Pareek, directly. Krish leads requirements and architecture; Dhairya leads data, forecasting and integrations. The people who scope the system are the people who build it, so nothing is lost in a hand-off between sales and engineering.

Do we need to replace the tools we already use?

No. Systems are built around your existing CRM, telephony, inbox and data. Where an off-the-shelf component is the right answer, we use it rather than building a platform you do not need.

How is our data handled?

Access and compliance rules are enforced inside the system, with audit logging where it matters. Where data is sensitive, systems can be deployed inside your own environment; a previous insurance deployment runs on-premises with encryption at rest.

Who owns what gets built?

You do. The code, configuration and documentation produced for your project are yours, and the system is handed over in a state your own team can run.

What if AI is not the right answer?

Then we will say so. Some problems are better solved with a simple rule, a form or a process change, and the scoping step is designed to find that out early.

What happens after launch?

Systems are monitored in production, thresholds are tuned against real usage, and ongoing support is agreed as part of the scope.
